2D orthomosaic mapping

A useful output, not just a drone flight.

Ordinary aerial photos are useful for context, but they are not the same thing as a mapped orthomosaic. For a mapping job, Plotbird captures a planned set of overlapping images and processes them into a single detailed aerial view.

That makes it much easier to understand a whole site at once: access routes, buildings, vegetation, tracks, paddocks and other visible features can all sit in one connected image instead of being scattered across individual photos.

Typical jobs are property-sized rather than giant survey programmes. The flight plan, altitude and processing settings are chosen around the area, level of detail and final use.

  • Property and lifestyle-block overview maps
  • Farm and paddock documentation
  • Small development or site records
  • Repeat imagery for visual change or progress
  • Orthomosaic exports for use in GIS or reports
Important: Plotbird provides visual mapping, imagery and photogrammetry. It does not replace licensed cadastral or engineering survey work where that is required.

Questions

Before the drone goes up.

How accurate is a Plotbird drone map?

Accuracy depends on the aircraft, flight geometry, processing and whether ground control is used. Plotbird will describe what an output is suitable for rather than presenting visual mapping as a licensed survey.

Can you map a whole farm?

Potentially, yes, depending on the farm size, airspace, terrain and the resolution required. Large areas may be better split into multiple missions or scoped at a higher altitude.

What do I receive?

The exact deliverables are agreed first. A typical mapping job can include a high-resolution orthomosaic image and, where useful, mapped overlays or GIS-ready exports.
