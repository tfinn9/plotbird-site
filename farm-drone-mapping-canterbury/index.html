Rural mapping

A useful output, not just a drone flight.

Farm imagery is most useful when it helps someone understand the land quickly. Plotbird can create broad property maps for context or higher-detail captures of a smaller area when buildings, tracks, plantings or other ground features matter more.

A finished orthomosaic can also become a base for simple overlays such as paddock labels or visible property features. That is often more useful than a folder full of disconnected drone photographs.

For jobs that do not need full mapping, straightforward aerial stills can document the farm, lifestyle block, access, buildings or seasonal conditions from above.

  • Whole-property aerial overviews
  • Paddock and block mapping
  • Buildings, tracks and access documentation
  • Lifestyle-block aerial imagery
  • Repeat captures for visible change over time
Important: Plotbird provides visual mapping, imagery and photogrammetry. It does not replace licensed cadastral or engineering survey work where that is required.

Questions

Before the drone goes up.

Can paddocks be labelled on the map?

Yes. Visible paddock or management areas can be digitised and labelled as a practical visual layer where that helps the job.

Is this crop-health or multispectral mapping?

Not currently. Plotbird's core service uses standard RGB aerial imagery for visual mapping and documentation rather than specialist multispectral agronomy analysis.

How large an area can you cover?

It depends on the required ground detail, local airspace and practical battery or access constraints. The job is scoped around the useful output rather than promising a fixed hectare limit.
